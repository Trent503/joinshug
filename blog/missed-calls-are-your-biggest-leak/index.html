Why your missed calls are your biggest leak

A missed call is not a delayed lead. It is a lost one, handed to a competitor whose only advantage was answering. Here is the arithmetic, worked out.

The arithmetic nobody runs

Every trade owner knows they miss calls. Almost none of them have put a number on it, because the number is unpleasant and the calls are invisible — a missed call that never leaves a voicemail leaves no trace at all.

So let us do it properly. Take a plumber with a $400 average ticket.

Four missed calls a week. That is conservative for a working operator with both hands occupied most of the day. Say half of those were real jobs rather than spam, wrong numbers, or vendors — two real opportunities a week.

Say you would have closed 80 percent of them, because someone calling a plumber has a problem and wants it solved. That is 1.6 jobs a week.

1.6 × $400 = $640 a week. $33,280 a year.

Now run it for a roofer at a $9,000 average ticket and a 25 percent close rate. Four missed calls, half real, is two opportunities. At 25 percent that is half a job a week. Half a job at $9,000 is $4,500 a week — $234,000 a year.

Those numbers look absurd, and the instinct is to argue with the inputs. Do that. Lower every assumption. Say you only miss two calls a week and only a third are real and you would only close half. The plumber is still losing nearly $7,000 a year and the roofer over $150,000, to a problem that costs nothing to fix.

The leak is not that the numbers are big. It is that they are invisible. You never see the job you did not know about.

Nobody leaves a voicemail anymore

The reason this leak is total rather than partial is behavioral, and it changed underneath the trades without much comment.

People used to leave voicemails. Now they do not. Voicemail feels slow, one-directional, and a bit awkward — you have to compose a message to a stranger and then wait. Younger homeowners in particular treat an unanswered call as a closed door rather than a message opportunity.

What they do instead is hang up and tap the next result. They already have the search results open. You were the first number. The second number is right there and takes one thumb movement.

Which means a missed call is not a delayed lead. It is a lost lead, immediately, to a specific competitor whose only advantage was being able to answer.

The window is about two minutes wide

Here is the part that makes this fixable.

The homeowner who just called you is still holding the phone. They have not dialed the next company yet — there is a gap between hanging up on you and deciding what to do next, and that gap is short but it is real.

If something arrives in that gap, you are still in the running. If it arrives twenty minutes later, they have already booked someone.

That is the entire mechanism. It is not persuasion. It is not a better sales script. It is occupying a two-minute window that you currently leave empty, and the only way to reliably occupy it is to have something send automatically, because you are still on the ladder.

It quietly makes your advertising worse

If you run Local Services Ads, this stops being just a lost-revenue problem and becomes a cost problem.

Google tracks whether you answer. Responsiveness feeds into how often your ads get shown, which means a poor answer rate reduces your impressions and raises what you effectively pay per lead. You are paying for calls you do not pick up, and then being shown less because you did not pick them up.

Same principle applies to your Google Business Profile more broadly. The signals Google uses to decide who to surface for local searches include how businesses actually behave, and a business that does not respond is a worse result for the person searching.

So the missed-call leak is not one leak. It is a lost job, plus a worse ad position, plus a weaker profile, all from the same root cause.

The fix is one text

Within seconds of a missed call, an automatic text goes to that number. Something like: Sorry we missed you — we're on a job. What do you need? Text back here and we'll get you sorted.

It works for three reasons that have nothing to do with cleverness.

It lands inside the window. Seconds, not minutes. They are still holding the phone.

It moves them to a channel that suits them better. Most people would rather text than talk. A text thread means they can describe the problem, send a photo of it, and get an answer without a phone conversation neither of you especially wanted.

It suits you better too. You can answer a text between jobs. You cannot answer a call from inside a crawlspace. The automation is not replacing you — it is holding the lead until you are free.

And it signals something the competitor who picked up did not: that you have your act together enough to have a system. For a homeowner deciding between two contractors they know nothing about, that is not nothing.

The objections worth answering

"It feels impersonal." Less personal than never responding at all. And the automated part is one message — after that it is you texting a customer like a normal person.

"I'll just answer more calls." You will not, and you should not. You are on a roof. The job you are doing is worth more than the call, and the answer is not to divide your attention — it is to stop making the choice matter.

"My customers are older, they don't text." Partly true, and it is a smaller share every year. The text costs nothing when it does not land, and the older customer who was going to leave a voicemail still can.

"I'd need to buy software." You need the capability, not a dashboard. We set it up on your existing number and you never log into anything. It is included at every tier rather than sold separately, because a website that generates calls you then drop is not actually a solution to anything.

Run your own numbers

Do not take my inputs. Use yours.

  1. Check your phone's recent calls for the last seven days and count the ones you did not answer.
  2. Subtract the spam, the vendors, and the wrong numbers. Be harsh about it.
  3. Multiply what is left by your average job value.
  4. Multiply that by the share of inbound callers you normally close.
  5. Multiply by 52.

Whatever that number is, it is the most expensive thing in your business that nobody has ever sent you an invoice for.

Then compare it to what it costs to fix. For most operators the recovered revenue from a single month covers the entire year of the tier it comes bundled in.

You already did the marketing that made that phone ring. You already earned the reputation that got you into consideration. The call arriving is the hard part, and it already happened. All that is left is not dropping it.
