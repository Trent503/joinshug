AI receptionists for contractors, explained without the hype

What an AI receptionist is, what it does on a real call, what it costs, where it beats an answering service, and where it is the wrong tool. Written for people who run home-service businesses, not for people who buy software.

By Trent Delgadillo · Founder of Shug · Updated August 2026

An AI receptionist is software that answers your business phone, has a real conversation with the caller, and finishes the call with an action — a booked appointment, a task created, or a transfer to you. For home-service businesses the point is not novelty; it is that the phone gets answered while you are on a roof, under a sink, or asleep.

What an AI receptionist actually is

An AI receptionist — also called an AI phone agent or AI answering service — answers an inbound call, speaks with the caller in natural conversation, and takes an action based on what it learns.

It is worth separating it from three things it gets confused with:

  • Voicemail records a message. Nothing happens until you listen to it.
  • An auto-attendant is the "press 1 for sales" menu. It routes, it does not converse.
  • A live answering service is people, working from a script you supply, who usually hand you a message.

An AI receptionist is the only one of the four that can both hold the conversation and complete the task at the end of it. That is the whole distinction, and every other difference follows from it.

How it handles a contractor's call

The mechanics are less mysterious than the marketing suggests. A call arrives. The AI answers as your business. It works through the questions your trade actually needs answered, and then it does one of three things: books the appointment, creates a task or message for you, or transfers the call.

The part that determines whether it is any good is not the AI. It is the setup. A generic agent that has not been told your services, your service area, your pricing posture and your definition of "urgent" will produce exactly the flat, robotic experience contractors are afraid of. One that has been configured properly sounds like a competent front desk that happens to be available at midnight.

When you are evaluating any provider, that is the question to press on: how much of this is configured around my business, and who does that work?

Why missed calls cost more in home services than elsewhere

In most industries an unanswered call is an inconvenience. In home services it is usually a lost job, for one structural reason: the caller has an urgent problem and a list of alternatives.

A homeowner with water coming through a ceiling, a furnace that quit in January, or a panel that smells hot is not going to leave a message and wait. They are working down a Google results page. Whoever answers gets the conversation, and in practice whoever gets the conversation gets the job — not because they were the best choice, but because the problem stopped being urgent once someone picked up.

This is why "answer the phone" outperforms most marketing spend for home-service businesses. You are not competing on being better. You are competing on being available.

Booking, qualifying and after-hours

Booking. A capable AI receptionist can put the appointment on the calendar or create the task in the system you already use. This is the feature that separates a real receptionist replacement from a fancy voicemail. Ask any provider to show you what actually lands on your side at the end of a call.

Qualifying. Qualification is just consistency — asking the same set of questions every time. What is the job, where is it, when do they need it, and the two or three things specific to your trade that decide whether it is worth your morning. Most owners ask maybe half of these when the phone rings mid-job. Software asks all of them every time.

After hours. This is where the maths gets easy. Evenings, weekends and holidays are hours you were never going to staff, and they are when a large share of emergency home-service calls happen. An AI receptionist covers them at the same cost as covering Tuesday morning.

How it compares with the alternatives

Short version: an answering service takes a message, an in-house hire costs a wage, voicemail loses the caller, and an AI receptionist answers 24/7 and can finish the job. The longer, table-based version — including where an AI receptionist is the wrong answer — is on the comparison page.

The honest caveat: if your call volume genuinely justifies a full-time person, hire the person. A good front-desk employee who knows your customers is better than any software on this page. The reason most one-truck and two-truck operations do not is that the phone does not ring enough to fill a wage, but rings far too much to answer from a ladder.

CRM and scheduling integrations

This is where you should be most sceptical of vendor claims, including ours, so here is Shug's actual position:

  • Jobber — a direct integration is in active development. It is not finished.
  • Housecall Pro and ServiceTitan — no direct integration today.
  • Everything else — Shug is configured during setup to book the appointment or create the task through the route your business already uses.

The general advice applies to every provider you look at: ask which integrations are live today rather than on a roadmap, and ask what happens on a call when the integration is not there. "Coming soon" has ended a lot of software trials.

How to judge one before you buy

Four tests, in the order that matters:

  • Call it yourself, as a customer. Not a scripted demo — call the number and describe a real job the way one of your customers would. If a provider will not give you a live line to call, that tells you something.
  • Ask what happens at the end of the call. A booked appointment, a task in your system, or a message you still have to action? These are very different products at similar prices.
  • Ask how the emergency rules work. You should be able to define what reaches you immediately. If everything either transfers or nothing does, it will not survive contact with a real week.
  • Ask what setup includes and who does it. Configuration is the difference between sounding like your company and sounding like a robot. Find out whether you are doing that work or they are.
