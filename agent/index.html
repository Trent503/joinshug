What Shug does on a call

Everything below is live today. Nothing here is a roadmap item.

  • Answers 24/7 as your business. Evenings, weekends, holidays, and the middle of the job you are standing on right now.
  • Handles common questions. What you do, where you work, roughly how you price, when you could come out.
  • Captures the lead properly. Name, number, address, what the job is, when they need it — asked in the same order every single time.
  • Qualifies it. The two or three questions specific to your trade that decide whether a job is worth your morning.
  • Books the appointment. Shug can book the appointment or create the task on your side so it lands where you already work.
  • Filters spam. Robocalls and cold-callers stop reaching your pocket.
  • Transfers urgent calls. On rules you define, not on a generic guess about what urgent means.

What Shug does not do

Worth being blunt about, because the gap between what AI is sold as and what it does is where trust dies.

  • It does not diagnose a problem or tell a caller whether something is safe.
  • It does not quote work sight-unseen. It gathers and books; you price.
  • It does not pretend to be you personally.
  • It does not make outbound sales calls.
  • It does not replace a great front-desk employee if you have the volume to justify one.

How the setup works, and why it's the whole game

The $198 is not an activation fee. It is the configuration work that decides whether Shug sounds like your company or like a robot, and it is the single biggest difference between AI receptionists that work and AI receptionists that get cancelled in month two.

What gets defined:

  • Your services, and the ones you do not want calls about.
  • Your service area, including the edges you would rather not drive to.
  • How you want calls handled — tone, what it offers, what it declines.
  • What counts as urgent enough to interrupt you, in your trade's actual language.
  • Where a booking should land so you will see it.

Straight answers

The objections, answered honestly.

Will my customers know it isn't me?

Shug answers as your business and handles the call the way a front desk would. It is not pretending to be you personally. If the caller needs you specifically, it can transfer them.

Will it sound robotic?

That is the right thing to be suspicious about, which is why there is a live demo line instead of a demo video. Call (503) 376-8729 and hear it yourself. The difference between a good AI receptionist and a bad one is almost entirely setup, and that is what the $198 covers.

Can it actually book a job on my calendar?

Yes. Shug can book an appointment or create the task on your side, so the job lands where you already work instead of sitting in a voicemail box you check at nine at night.

Can it qualify a lead, or does it just take a message?

It qualifies. Shug asks what the job is, where it is, when they need it, and the questions specific to your trade that decide whether it is worth your morning. Consistency is the advantage: it asks all of them every time.

Can it handle after-hours calls?

Yes, and that is where most of the value is. Shug answers at 2am, on Sundays and on holidays, following the same rules you set for a Tuesday afternoon, including which calls should reach you immediately.

Will it work with my CRM?

A direct Jobber integration is in active development and is not finished. There is no direct Housecall Pro or ServiceTitan integration today. Where there is no direct integration, Shug is set up during onboarding to book the appointment or create the task through the route your business already uses.

What happens after 120 minutes?

120 minutes a month is included in the $99. That covers normal call volume for most one-truck and two-truck operations. If you consistently run past it, we will talk about it before you get a surprise, not after.

What if it gets something wrong?

You decide up front what counts as urgent enough to reach you, and Shug transfers those calls instead of trying to handle them. Setup is where that gets defined: your services, your service area, how you want calls handled, and what should always come straight to you.

Why not just hire a receptionist?

If your phone rings enough to keep someone busy all day, hire the person. A good front-desk employee beats software. The problem for most one-truck and two-truck operations is that the phone does not ring enough to justify a wage but rings far too much to answer from a ladder.

What does the $198 setup cover?

Setting Shug up for your business specifically: your services, your service area, how you want calls handled, and what counts as urgent enough to reach you. That is the work that makes it sound like your company instead of a generic answering service.
