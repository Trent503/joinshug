What most trade websites get wrong

Most trade websites were built to be looked at. Yours needs to be built to be used.

Here is what usually happened. You paid someone eighteen hundred dollars three years ago. They built it on a page builder, stacked twenty plugins on top, and handed it over. Nobody has touched it since. It loads in six seconds on a phone. The gallery is stock photos of somebody else's work. Your phone number is an image, so nobody can tap it.

None of that is a design problem. It is a plumbing problem — the site is not connected to anything. A homeowner lands on it, waits, gets bored, hits back, and calls the guy who ranked below you.

The uncomfortable part is that this is not a small leak. If your site gets 300 visits a month and converts at 1 percent, that is three calls. At 5 percent it is fifteen. Same traffic, same trade, same quality of work. The only thing that changed is whether the site did its job.

What we build instead

A Shug site does four things, in this order.

It loads before they lose patience. No page builder, no plugin stack, no framework. Static files, compressed images, and a page that paints in under a second on a phone on cell service. Speed is not a vanity metric here — it is the difference between a call and a bounce.

It answers the question they came with. Do you do my kind of job. Do you work in my area. What does it roughly cost. Are you real. Every one of those gets answered above the fold, in plain language, without making anyone scroll to find it.

It makes contacting you the easiest thing on the page. Click-to-call in the header on mobile. A short form that asks for a name and a number and nothing else. Quote capture that does not demand an address before it will talk to you. Every extra field you add is a percentage of leads you throw away.

It sends the lead somewhere that acts on it. A form that emails an inbox you check on Sundays is not a lead system. Yours texts you the moment it comes in, and if you miss the call that follows, the missed-call text-back catches it.

How a real site goes live in 24 hours

The 24 hours is real, and it is not a trick. Here is the actual sequence.

We start with a 15-minute call. Not a pitch — a working call where I get your services, your service area, the jobs you want more of, and the jobs you would rather stop taking. That last one matters more than people expect. Most trade sites are built to attract every job, which is why they attract the wrong ones.

You send photos. Real ones. Your truck, your crew, finished work, the before-and-afters on your phone right now. Ten good photos beat a hundred stock images and they are the single biggest trust signal on the page.

We build. You get a link the next day. You mark up anything you want changed, we change it, it goes live. Domain pointed, SSL on, forms tested with a real submission to your phone.

What makes that possible is that the hard part is already solved. We are not inventing a layout. We know what converts for trades because we ran one — the whole reason Shug exists is that I built an exterior cleaning company to $243K a year and had to figure this out the expensive way.
