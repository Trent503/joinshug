Homeowners do not compare workmanship

They cannot. They have no way to judge whether your flashing detail is better than the next guy's. So they compare what they can see — who shows up first, who has more reviews, and who calls back fastest.

That is not cynicism about your work. It is just how buying a service you cannot evaluate works. The best operator in the county loses to the third-best one who ranks above him. That gap is not a quality problem. It is a visibility problem, and it is fixable.

The other thing worth being honest about: word of mouth does not exempt you from this. Every referral you get still types your name into Google before they call. If what comes back is a thin profile with four reviews from 2019 and no website, that referral cools off. You earned the trust and then lost it at the last step.

Where local search is actually won

There are three surfaces, and they do not carry equal weight.

The map pack. Three businesses, a map, and the top of the phone screen. This is where the majority of local service clicks go. Ranking here comes down to proximity, prominence, and relevance — how close you are to the searcher, how well-known and well-reviewed you are, and how clearly your profile says you do the thing they searched for.

Organic results. The blue links under the map. This is where your service pages live, and where the longer-tail searches land — the ones with buying intent attached. Someone searching a specific service plus a specific problem is much further down the path than someone searching your trade plus your city.

Local Services Ads. Above everything, Google-guaranteed, pay-per-lead. That is a separate service and a separate budget, but it sits on the same foundation. A weak profile makes your ads more expensive.

What we actually do

Google Business Profile, properly. Most profiles are half-filled. Categories wrong or too few. No services listed. No products. Hours missing. Photos from whenever the account was created. We fill every field, pick the primary category deliberately, list your services individually, and keep photos current. This is the cheapest ranking work that exists and almost nobody does it.

Service pages that deserve to rank. One page per service you want more of, written for the search that leads to it. Not a paragraph on a combined services page — a real page with the question answered, the process explained, and the pricing range addressed honestly. Google ranks pages, not businesses.

Service area coverage. Pages for the areas you genuinely work in, built with real content about working there — not the same paragraph with the city name swapped. Doorway pages get filtered out, and they make your whole site look spammy.

Reviews as a ranking input. Review count and recency both feed the map pack. This is why automated review requests are bundled in rather than sold separately — they are not a nice-to-have, they are half of local SEO.

Technical hygiene. Schema markup, clean site structure, fast pages, no broken links, correct canonical URLs. This is table stakes and it comes with the site.

Rank tracking and monthly reporting. You get the actual positions for the terms that matter, tracked over time. Not a traffic chart. Where you rank, for what, in which areas, and what moved.

What we will not do

No fake locations. No mailbox addresses in cities you do not serve. No review gating, no bought reviews, no review markup for reviews that do not exist. No private blog networks or bought links.

All of that works until it does not, and when it stops working it takes the business down with it. A manual action on your Google Business Profile can remove you from the map entirely. That is not a risk worth a temporary bump.

Built for your trade

Search behavior is trade-specific. Plumbing is dominated by emergency intent where speed of response beats everything. Roofing spikes after storms and carries insurance-claim searches that behave nothing like normal service queries. HVAC swings hard with the seasons. Landscaping and exterior cleaning are won on recurring and route density, not on one big job.

Straight answers

Questions operators ask about SEO.

How long does local SEO actually take?

Google Business Profile work can move the map pack in two to six weeks. Organic rankings for service pages take three to six months to settle. Anyone promising page one in thirty days is either lying or targeting keywords nobody searches.

What is the map pack and why does it matter more than the blue links?

The map pack is the three-business block with the map above the normal results. On a phone it takes up most of the first screen. For local service searches it gets the majority of the clicks, which means ranking fourth in the map pack is closer to invisible than to second place.

Do I need a physical address to rank locally?

You need a valid business address that Google can verify, but it can be a service-area business — you can hide the address and set a radius. What you cannot do is invent locations or rent a mailbox in a city you do not serve. Google catches it and the penalty is worse than never ranking.

What happens if I stop?

The rankings you earned do not vanish overnight, but they decay. Competitors keep publishing, keep collecting reviews, and keep getting fresher. SEO is a position you hold, not a project you finish.
