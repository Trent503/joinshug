Ads are the fastest lever and the easiest one to break

SEO takes months. Ads take a day. That is the whole appeal, and it is real — you can go from nothing to leads in your phone by the end of the week.

It is also the fastest way to set money on fire. Most trade owners who tell me they tried Google Ads and it did not work are describing the same situation: a campaign somebody set up once, pointed at a slow homepage, with broad match turned on, bidding on searches that were never going to become jobs. The ads worked fine. Everything downstream of them did not.

So before anything else, the honest version: ads are the last thing we turn on, not the first. They sit on top of a site that converts and a profile that already ranks. Without those, you are renting traffic and leaking it.

Local Services Ads first

For most trades, Local Services Ads are the better first dollar. They sit above the map pack, above the regular ads, above everything. They carry the Google Guaranteed badge. And they charge per lead instead of per click, which means you are not paying for someone who clicked, waited two seconds, and left.

Getting in requires a background check, license verification, and proof of insurance. It takes a couple of weeks and it is genuinely annoying. It is also a moat — every competitor who cannot be bothered is a competitor who is not above you.

Once you are in, the levers are your budget, your service categories, your service area, and your review profile. That last one is why automated review requests matter here too. Your LSA ranking is heavily influenced by review count and rating, so the automation that fills your profile also lowers your cost per lead.

The other thing that moves LSA cost: answering the phone. Google tracks whether you pick up. Miss enough calls and you get shown less. Which means missed-call text-back is not just a lead-recovery tool here — it is ad-performance infrastructure.

Search campaigns, when they earn it

Regular search ads make sense once LSAs are running and you want more volume or coverage on searches LSAs do not serve. Done properly that means exact and phrase match on terms with real intent, a negative keyword list that grows every week, ad copy that names the trade and the area, and a landing page built for that specific search rather than your homepage.

Call tracking on every campaign. Conversion tracking wired to actual booked jobs where your CRM allows it, not to form fills. A form fill is not a job.

The math we do before you spend a dollar

This is the part most agencies skip, and it is the only part that decides whether ads are a good idea for you.

Take your average job value. Multiply by your close rate on inbound leads. That is what a lead is worth to you. If your average roof is $9,000 and you close one in four, a lead is worth $2,250 in revenue — a $200 lead is obviously fine. If you run exterior cleaning at a $350 average ticket and close half, a lead is worth $175, and a $90 lead only works if your margin holds.

Then subtract your cost of delivery. Revenue is not profit, and a lead cost that looks fine against revenue can be underwater against margin.

If the numbers do not work, I will say so on the call and we will spend the money on SEO instead. I would rather lose the upsell than sell you a channel that loses you money.

Straight answers

Questions operators ask about ads.

What is the difference between Local Services Ads and regular Google Ads?

Local Services Ads sit above everything, carry the Google Guaranteed badge, and charge you per lead instead of per click. Regular search ads sit below them and charge per click. LSAs are usually the better first dollar for a trade because you pay for a phone call, not for a tire-kicker who bounced.

What should I budget?

Enough that the math works, which depends on your ticket size. If your average job is $400 and you close one in three leads, a $75 lead is profitable. If your average job is $180, it is not. We work that out on the call before you spend anything, and if ads are the wrong move for your numbers I will tell you.

Do I need Google Guaranteed?

For Local Services Ads, yes — it requires a background check, license verification, and insurance verification. It takes a couple of weeks. It is also a real trust signal, so it is worth doing even though it is a hassle.

Can I run ads without a good website?

You can. You will just pay more for worse results. Ads send traffic to a page — if that page is slow or unclear, you paid for the click and got nothing. Fix the site first. That is why ads are the top tier and not the entry one.

Who owns the ad account?

You do. It is set up under your business, with your billing. If you leave, the account, the history, and the data stay with you. Agencies that hold the account hostage are the reason this needs saying.

Is the ad spend included in the price?

No. The $1,297 covers management, the site, SEO, and everything below it. Ad spend goes directly to Google on your card. Anyone bundling spend into a flat fee is either marking it up or capping it quietly.
