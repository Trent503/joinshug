Four ways to answer the phone. Only one books the job while you're on a roof.

Every home-service business ends up choosing one of four options for inbound calls: an AI receptionist, a live answering service, hiring someone, or letting it ring out to voicemail. Here is the honest version of that comparison, including where an AI receptionist is the wrong answer.

Short version: a live answering service takes a message, an in-house receptionist costs a wage, voicemail loses the caller, and an AI receptionist answers 24/7 and can book the appointment itself. Shug is the AI receptionist option, at $99/mo plus a one-time $198 setup.

If your call volume is high enough to keep a person busy all day, or your calls are genuinely complex and non-repeating, hire a person. Most one-truck and two-truck operations are not in that situation.

AI receptionist vs answering service

The real difference is what you are holding at the end of the call.

A live answering service is people answering a phone from a script you provide. They are polite, they are human, and in most cases the output is a message: a name, a number, and a rough idea of what the caller wanted. You still have to call that person back, and you are usually calling them back after they have already called the next company on Google.

An AI receptionist handles the conversation to the end. Shug answers as your business, works through the common questions, and can book the appointment or create the task on your side rather than handing you a note about it. For a contractor, the practical test is simple: does the option you are paying for reduce the number of callbacks you owe at the end of the day, or just organise them?

Where a live service still wins: unusual, emotionally difficult, or highly variable calls where a human ear is worth paying for.

AI receptionist vs hiring a receptionist

A person is better at the job. A person is also a wage.

An in-house receptionist knows your business, hears the tone of a caller, and handles the strange call that no configuration anticipated. If your phone rings often enough to keep someone busy for most of a working day, or you need someone at a physical counter, hire the person. Nothing here beats that.

The problem for most one-truck and two-truck operations is that the phone does not ring enough to justify a full wage, but it rings far too much to keep answering it from a ladder. That gap is where an AI receptionist fits — not as a replacement for a great front-desk person, but as the thing that covers the hours you were never going to staff.

Compare honestly: take the hourly rate a receptionist actually commands in your market, add payroll tax and the hours you would need covered evenings and weekends, then set that against $99 a month. Do the arithmetic with your own numbers.

AI receptionist vs voicemail

Voicemail is the option most home-service businesses are actually using, whether or not they would describe it that way.

A homeowner with a leak, a dead furnace, or a roof that just failed is not in a patient frame of mind. When the call is not answered, the realistic next step is not a voicemail — it is the next result on Google. Voicemail does not lose the call politely; it hands the call to a competitor.

This is the comparison that matters most, because it is the one nearly everyone is defaulting into. Every other option on this page is better than it.

Where an AI receptionist is the wrong choice

We would rather say this plainly than have you find out in month two.

  • Your calls are long, unpredictable, and rarely repeat — an AI is set up around patterns, and you do not have any.
  • You need someone physically present at a desk or counter.
  • Your call volume genuinely justifies a full-time person, and you would rather have the person.
  • You are not willing to spend the setup conversation defining how calls should be handled. Setup is what makes it sound like your company rather than a generic robot, and skipping it produces exactly the experience people fear.

Is an AI receptionist the same thing as an answering service?

No. A traditional answering service is staffed by people who take a message and pass it on. An AI receptionist is software that answers in your business's voice, handles the whole conversation, and can book the appointment or create the task itself. The difference that matters to a contractor is what happens at the end of the call: a message you still have to act on, or a job already on the calendar.
