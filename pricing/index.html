How to pick a tier

The honest version, without the upsell.

Start on The Template if you have no real website. This is most people. You cannot rank a site that does not exist and you cannot run ads to a page that does not convert. Get the frontend right, plug the missed-call leak, and start collecting reviews automatically. That alone changes the month.

Move to The Foundation when the site is working and you want position. This is the tier most operators end up on. Local SEO is the compounding one — it takes three to six months to settle and then it keeps paying without you spending more. If you are going to be in business in a year, this is where the money goes furthest.

Take The Monopoly when your ticket size justifies paid leads. Run the math before you do. Average job value times your inbound close rate is what a lead is worth to you. If that number is comfortably above what a lead costs in your market, ads make sense. If it is not, stay on The Foundation and I will tell you so.

What "one job a month" actually means

The pricing is set against job value on purpose. At $297 a month, one small job covers the whole year's difference for most trades. At $1,297, one good roof or one system replacement covers several months.

That framing is not a sales trick, it is the only sane way to evaluate this. Marketing that costs less than one job and produces more than one job is not an expense you need to agonize over. Marketing that costs three jobs and produces two is, no matter how good the deck looked.

What every tier includes

  • A custom site, live in 24 hours, with hosting, SSL, backups, and maintenance
  • Unlimited edits — send a text, no change orders, no hourly billing
  • All four automations — missed-call text back, lead notifications, review requests, and past-customer re-engagement
  • You own the site, the domain, and the customer list
  • Month to month with a 30-day money-back guarantee

The automations are bundled rather than sold separately because they are not optional. A site that converts and then drops the call is still a leaky business.

What we do not charge for

No setup fee. No build fee. No onboarding fee. No cancellation fee. No charge for edits, no charge for a call, no charge for the blueprint call that decides whether any of this is right for you.

The only thing billed outside the monthly is ad spend on The Monopoly, and that goes straight to Google on your own card in your own account.

Straight answers

Questions about the money.

Is there a setup fee or a build cost?

No. The price you see is the whole price. There is no build fee, no onboarding fee, and no charge to launch. The site is included in the monthly, which is why there is no contract holding you to it.

What is not included?

Ad spend. On The Monopoly we manage your Google Local Services Ads, but the ad budget goes directly to Google on your card. Anyone bundling spend into a flat monthly fee is marking it up or capping it quietly.

Can I change tiers later?

Any time, in either direction. Most operators start on The Template to get the site live, then move up once they want the search position.

What happens if I cancel?

You keep the website, the domain, and the customer list. We hand it all over cleanly. It is month to month, so canceling is one text — no exit fee and no notice period.

How does the money-back guarantee work?

Thirty days from your first payment. If you do not believe it is worth the money, say so and you get a full refund. No forms and no argument. The full terms are on the guarantee page.

Why is it this cheap?

Because there is no account manager, no office, and no sales team taking a cut. It is a tight operation run by someone who came out of the trades, and the pricing reflects the overhead rather than what the market will bear.
