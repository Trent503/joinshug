Pressure washing

Half your quotes could be given from a photo. None of them get given from a ladder.

An AI receptionist for pressure washing and exterior cleaning businesses. Shug answers while you are on the wand, captures square footage, surface and access, and books the walkthrough — or the job itself.

What it does for an exterior cleaning company: answers 24/7 through the spring and autumn rush, captures surface type, rough size and access, sorts one-off cleans from recurring contracts, books estimates, and stops quote calls going to voicemail while you are running equipment.

You cannot hear a phone over a pressure washer

This is the most literal version of the missed-call problem in any trade.

A surface cleaner at full throttle is loud enough that you will not hear a ring, will not feel a buzz through wet gear, and would not want to answer with a wand in your hand if you did. So the calls stack up, and you return them at six o'clock — to people who booked someone else at two.

The work itself is also unusually quotable over the phone compared with other trades. A driveway, a two-storey vinyl house, a commercial parking lot: with the right three questions you know most of what you need. That combination — easy to qualify, impossible to answer live — is exactly where an AI receptionist earns its money.

What Shug asks an exterior cleaning caller

  • What surface. Concrete, vinyl siding, brick, roof, deck, fence. Different equipment, different chemistry, different price.
  • Roughly how big. Square footage, number of storeys, or something concrete like a two-car driveway. Homeowners cannot estimate area, so the question has to be asked in objects.
  • Is it a soft-wash job. Roofs and painted siding are not pressure jobs, and finding that out on the phone rather than on arrival protects both the surface and your margin.
  • Water access and power. The two things that quietly turn a profitable job into a bad afternoon.
  • Gate width and pets, for back gardens. Small questions that decide whether your rig actually fits.
  • One-off or recurring. Sorts a driveway clean from a commercial contract that recurs quarterly — a completely different conversation.

Season, weather, and the calls that all arrive in one week

Exterior cleaning has a demand curve driven entirely by the calendar and the forecast.

The first warm dry weekend of spring produces a fortnight of enquiries in about seventy-two hours. Autumn does something similar for moss, gutters and pre-winter cleans. In between there are quiet weeks where the phone barely moves. Staffing for the peak is impossible and staffing for the average means missing the peak, which is where the year's money is.

Shug costs the same in the dead of January as it does in the first week of May.

Photos, and the limits of what it will quote

Shug will capture the detail and book the job or the walkthrough. It will not give a firm price on a surface nobody has looked at.

If your process is to quote from photos, it can capture the caller's details and set the expectation that you will text them for pictures — which is faster and more accurate than guessing on a call, and keeps the pricing decision with the person who has to do the work.

Questions from this trade

Can it quote a driveway over the phone?

It can capture everything a quote needs — surface, rough size, access, and whether it is a soft-wash job — and book the work or the walkthrough. It will not give a firm price on a surface nobody has looked at, because that is how a profitable job stops being profitable.

Will it know the difference between pressure washing and soft washing?

It asks the questions that surface the difference: what the surface is, whether it is a roof or painted siding, and how many storeys. Setup is where you define which jobs you take, which you decline, and how each should be handled.

What happens during the spring rush?

Every caller gets answered rather than queued behind one person with a phone. That is the week an exterior cleaning business either captures its year or watches it go to whoever picked up.
