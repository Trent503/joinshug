Roofing

When the storm hits, the roofer who answers gets the street.

An AI receptionist for roofing contractors. Shug answers every call as your business, captures what a roof inspection actually needs, and books it — including the forty calls that arrive in the six hours after a hailstorm.

What it does for a roofing company: answers 24/7 through storm surges, captures the address and what the homeowner is seeing, separates active leaks from routine estimates, books inspections, and handles insurance questions without pretending to be an adjuster.

Roofing demand doesn't arrive evenly. It arrives all at once.

Most trades get a steady trickle of calls. Roofing gets a flat line and then a vertical one.

A hailstorm or a windstorm moves through, and for the next two days every roofer in the county gets more calls than they can physically answer. The work is real, the margin is real, and the difference between a good quarter and a wasted one is decided by who picked up the phone while everyone else was on a roof looking at the last customer's damage.

You cannot staff for that. Hiring a receptionist for the two weeks a year it matters is not a business plan, and neither is trying to answer forty calls between tear-off and dry-in. That surge is precisely the shape of problem software is good at.

What Shug asks a roofing caller

  • Address first. Roofing is a street-by-street business after a storm. Knowing which neighbourhood a call came from is half of routing your week.
  • What they can actually see. Missing shingles, granules in the gutters, a ceiling stain, daylight in the attic. Homeowners describe roofs badly, so the questions have to be concrete.
  • Is water coming in right now. This is the line between an inspection you book for Thursday and a call that should reach you tonight. You define which side gets transferred.
  • Age of the roof, if they know it. Separates a repair conversation from a replacement conversation before you drive out.
  • Whether they've filed a claim. Not so Shug can advise on it — so you know what conversation you are walking into.
  • Whether anyone else has been out. Tells you if you are first or fifth, which changes how you handle the appointment.

Insurance calls, handled without giving advice

A large share of roofing calls involve insurance, and that is a place where a badly configured AI could do real damage.

Shug does not interpret policies, estimate claim value, advise on deductibles, or tell a homeowner whether their damage is covered. It captures whether a claim exists, the carrier if the homeowner offers it, and any adjuster appointment date — then books your inspection. The judgement stays with you, which is where it is legally and practically supposed to sit.

The emergency tarp call at 11pm

Active water intrusion is the one roofing call that genuinely cannot wait, and it usually arrives at the worst hour.

You set the rule: water actively coming in transfers to you, day or night. Everything else — missing shingles after the wind, a neighbour's roofer knocking on doors, a two-year-old stain someone finally decided to deal with — gets booked into a normal inspection slot. That single distinction is most of what separates a roofing phone that works from one that either wakes you up constantly or never wakes you at all.

Questions from this trade

Can it handle forty calls in one afternoon after a storm?

Yes. That is the situation where an AI receptionist has the clearest advantage over any other option, because it does not queue callers behind each other the way one person answering a phone has to. Every caller gets answered and captured.

Will it give homeowners advice about their insurance claim?

No, and it should not. Shug captures whether a claim exists, the carrier if the homeowner offers it, and any adjuster appointment date, then books your inspection. It does not interpret policies, estimate claim value, or tell anyone whether damage is covered.

What about the 11pm call with water coming through a ceiling?

You set that as a transfer rule during setup. Active water intrusion reaches you directly, day or night. Routine damage and estimate requests get booked into normal inspection slots instead of waking you up.
