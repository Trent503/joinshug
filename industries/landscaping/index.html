Two businesses wearing one name

Landscaping is really two businesses, and most sites blur them into mush.

There is recurring maintenance — mowing, cleanups, fertilization, snow. Low ticket, predictable, and the thing that keeps your crew paid in a slow month. And there is design-build — patios, retaining walls, irrigation, full installs. High ticket, lumpy, and worth ten maintenance accounts each.

They are bought completely differently. A maintenance customer wants reliability and a price. A design-build customer wants proof you can execute something they can picture. Selling both from the same page means selling neither well.

Where landscaping companies leak money

Everything is priced as a one-off. A homeowner searching for lawn mowing is a recurring contract that has not been asked yet. If your site sells a mow instead of a season, you converted a customer into a transaction.

The design-build portfolio is invisible. This work photographs as well as anything in the trades and most companies have it buried three clicks deep, or not on the site at all.

Route density gets ignored. Same problem as exterior cleaning and it is worse here because the tickets are smaller. Six lawns on one street is a profitable morning. Six lawns across the county is a loss dressed up as a full schedule.

Season open is left to chance. The first warm week produces a rush of searching. Companies that reach out to last year's list before that week fill their route. Companies that wait get whatever is left.

Snow and off-season work is disconnected. If you plow, that is the same customer list twice a year. Most companies market it as if it were a separate business to separate people.

What we build for landscaping

A site that separates the two businesses. Maintenance pages built around a season, not a visit — with plan tiers, what is included, and a real price range. Design-build pages built around the portfolio, with the work large and the process explained, because a $20,000 patio needs a different page than a weekly mow.

And review requests after every job through Jobber or Housecall Pro, which for a trade sold on reliability is the proof that actually converts.

Pricing a season instead of a visit

The single biggest change most landscaping companies can make is how the offer is presented, and it costs nothing to make.

A per-visit price invites a per-visit decision. Every week the customer gets a small opportunity to reconsider, compare, or skip. A season price removes that entirely — one decision in March covers thirty visits, and the customer stops thinking about it, which is what they wanted anyway.

It also changes what you can plan. A route full of seasonal agreements is a revenue number you know in advance, which means you can hire against it, buy equipment against it, and stop guessing at July from inside February.

The objection is always that customers will not commit. Some will not, and those stay per-visit. But most homeowners are not resisting the commitment — they were never offered it clearly, because the website listed services instead of plans. Present three tiers with what is included and a real price range, and a meaningful share take the middle one without being sold to.

Straight answers

Questions landscapers ask.

Should I sell contracts or per-visit?

Contracts, wherever the customer will take them. Predictable revenue is worth more than a slightly higher per-visit rate, and it makes your route plannable. The site should present a season as the default and per-visit as the exception.

Do I need separate pages for lawn care and hardscaping?

Yes. They are different searches, different budgets, and different decisions. One combined services page ranks for neither and converts badly for both.

How do I get more jobs on the same street?

Neighborhood-level service pages plus re-engagement timed so existing customers in that area rebook in the same window. Density is something you build deliberately.

Is snow removal worth marketing separately?

If you offer it, yes. It is a different search with different urgency, and it is the cheapest way to keep your list warm through the months when nothing is growing.

I am booked solid already. Why market?

Being booked with the wrong work is not the same as being profitable. Marketing lets you replace scattered low-margin jobs with dense routes and contracts, which is usually more money for less driving.
