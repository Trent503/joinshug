The electrical phone problem is a triage problem

Most trades have one kind of urgent call. Electricians have a spectrum, and the caller cannot tell you where on it they are.

"My outlets stopped working" might be a tripped GFCI that can wait until Thursday. It might also be the first sign of something that should not wait an hour. The homeowner describing it does not know the difference, and neither does a voicemail box. Meanwhile the calls that pay for your year — panel upgrades, service upgrades, EV chargers, generator installs — arrive sounding calm and get treated as less important precisely because nothing is on fire.

That is the actual failure. Not that you miss calls, but that the calls you miss are sorted by nothing at all.

What Shug is set up to do with an electrical call

  • Power loss and partial outages. Captures what is out, whether it is the whole house or one circuit, and whether the neighbours have power too — the question that separates your problem from the utility's.
  • Anything that sounds unsafe. Burning smells, sparking, smoke, a panel that is warm, water anywhere near electrical. You define these at setup and Shug transfers rather than handles.
  • Panel and service upgrades. Current service size if the caller knows it, what they are adding, the age of the home, and a booked estimate.
  • EV charger installs. Vehicle, desired charger, where the panel sits relative to the parking spot. The three things that decide whether the job is simple or a trench.
  • Generator enquiries. Whole-home or partial, fuel, and whether they are calling in a storm or planning ahead.
  • Inspection repair lists. Realtor or homeowner, the closing date, and the list itself.
  • Troubleshooting calls. Books the diagnostic and sets the expectation that diagnosis is its own visit, so you are not arguing about it on the doorstep.

Why after-hours matters more in electrical than in most trades

A homeowner who loses power at 9pm is not going to wait until morning to start calling. They will work down the Google results until somebody picks up, and the company that picks up gets the call even if you are better, closer and cheaper.

The same is true in the other direction. Storm nights generate a week of work in four hours, and the businesses that capture it are the ones whose phone was answered. Shug answers at 9pm, at 2am, and on the Sunday of a long weekend, and follows the same rules you set for a Tuesday afternoon.

What it does not do

Shug does not diagnose faults, does not price work over the phone, and does not tell a caller whether something is safe. Those are your judgements and they need eyes on the problem. Shug's job is to make sure the call reaches you as a booked appointment or a live transfer instead of a missed call.

Straight answers

Questions electricians ask

Can it tell the difference between a dead outlet and a burning smell?

That is exactly what setup is for. You decide which words and situations mean transfer this to me now — burning smell, sparking, smoke, water in a panel — and Shug transfers those instead of trying to handle them. Everything routine keeps going through the normal flow.

Can it quote a panel upgrade?

No, and it should not. Shug captures what the caller has, what they are trying to add, and when they need it, then books the estimate. Pricing a service upgrade sight-unseen is how electricians lose money, so Shug is set up to gather and schedule rather than to guess.

What about calls from realtors with an inspection deadline?

Those are date-driven, and Shug captures the date. A realtor calling about an inspection repair list needs to know you can be there before closing, and the useful thing to hand you is the address, the list, and the deadline — not a voicemail you hear that evening.
