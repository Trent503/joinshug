If you run a home-service business in Portland, Beaverton, Hillsboro, Tigard, Lake Oswego, Wilsonville, Sherwood, Oregon City, Gresham or anywhere else in the metro, that is the whole local connection. It is a real one, and it is not more than that.

What the phone actually does in this metro

A few things about running a trade business here shape the call pattern in ways that are worth naming:

  • The wet season runs long. Roughly October through May, which concentrates gutter, roofing, moss and drainage calls into the months when nobody wants to be on a ladder answering a phone.
  • Ice storms create a week of work in one night. When the region loses power, electricians, tree services and restoration contractors get more calls in a few hours than they get in a normal month — almost all of them outside business hours. Whoever answers, wins that week.
  • Smoke season moved HVAC demand. Late-summer wildfire smoke has made filtration and indoor air quality a live conversation for homeowners here in a way it was not fifteen years ago.
  • The inner-city housing stock is old. Which means panel upgrades, knob-and-tube questions, sewer scopes and pre-sale repair lists are ordinary calls, not exotic ones — and they arrive with real-estate deadlines attached.
  • The metro spans two states. Service areas that cross into Vancouver and Clark County need that boundary handled on the call rather than discovered on arrival.

None of that requires a local receptionist. It requires the phone to be answered, and the caller's address and deadline captured, at the moment they call.

Licensing and what Shug will not say on your behalf

Oregon requires most home-improvement and construction contractors to be licensed with the Construction Contractors Board, and homeowners here are more likely than average to ask about it on a first call.

Shug can confirm the facts you give it during setup — that you are licensed, bonded and insured, and your CCB number if you want it stated. It will not make claims about your licensing that you have not supplied, quote a job, or give advice about permits or code. Those are yours.
